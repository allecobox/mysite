Artwork Setup: Get Your File Print-Ready

Most delays in a packaging order are not production delays — they are artwork delays. This is the checklist we run before a file goes on press, so you can fix the common problems before you send it.

By AllecoBox · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

Minimum viable file: AI, PDF or CDR · CMYK · 300 dpi at final size · 3 mm bleed · 5 mm safe area · fonts outlined · cut and crease lines on a separate layer. That's it. Everything else is refinement.

File format: what we can open

FormatAccepted?Notes
AI (Illustrator, CS5+)BestLayered, editable, spot colours preserved — our preferred format
PDF (print-ready, layered)YesFlatten transparency, embed or outline fonts, keep 3 mm bleed
CDR (CorelDRAW)YesConvert text to curves before export
PSD (flattened, 300 dpi)Yes, limitedFine for images; text layers must be rasterised at 300 dpi
JPG / PNGNot for printNo bleed, no spot colours, usually 72 dpi — we can rebuild, but it costs time
Canva / Figma exportOnly as PDF with bleedExport at 300 dpi with bleed; check that CMYK conversion did not shift brand colours

Resolution: 300 dpi at final size, not at screen size

A 1000 px logo looks sharp on a monitor and blurs on a 200 mm box. The rule is 300 dpi at the final printed dimensions: a 200 × 150 mm panel needs about 2,360 × 1,770 px. Vector art (AI, EPS, SVG) has no resolution limit and is always preferred for logos and type.

Colour: CMYK, and know your brand numbers

  • Convert RGB to CMYK before sending. Bright RGB greens and oranges cannot be reproduced in CMYK and will shift duller — better to see the shift in the design stage than on a printed box.
  • Give Pantone numbers if the colour must match. "Brand orange #F37961" is a screen colour; "Pantone 7417 C" is a print instruction. We match Pantone references when spot colours are quoted.
  • Rich black for large black areas: C60 M40 Y40 K100, not 100% K alone, which prints grey-brown on large solids.
  • Total ink coverage under 300% on coated stock, or the sheet will not dry cleanly.

Bleed, safe area and folds

ZoneDistanceRule
Bleed3 mm past the cut lineExtend every background and photo — never leave it flush
Safe area5 mm inside the cut lineAll text, logos, barcodes, legal copy
Fold clearance3 mm either side of a creaseNo small type or thin rules across a fold
Glue flap12–15 mm stripKeep clear of design — it is glued and hidden

Layers and spot colour naming

Printers read your file by layer name. Use these and nothing gets printed by accident:

  • "Dieline" layer — cut and crease lines, set to a spot colour named Dieline, set to 100% magenta or a dedicated spot, and marked non-printing. This layer must never appear on the finished box.
  • "Artwork" layer — everything visible.
  • "Foil" layer — the exact areas to be stamped, as a solid spot colour named Foil.
  • "SpotUV" layer — areas to be varnished, solid spot colour named SpotUV.
  • "White" layer — only if printing on kraft or transparent stock, where white ink is laid down first.

Our dieline file already includes these layer names — keep them and drop your artwork into the Artwork layer, rather than building a new file from scratch.

Barcodes and QR codes

Barcodes fail for one reason: not enough quiet zone. Leave at least 4 mm of empty space on all sides of an EAN/UPC barcode, and print it at the recommended magnification (usually 80–100%) in a dark colour on a light background. Never place a barcode across a fold, over a curve, or on a glossy spot-UV patch — scanners need a matte, flat surface.

Pre-flight checklist — run this before sending

  • File is AI, PDF or CDR (not JPG/PNG)
  • 300 dpi at final size, images embedded
  • Colour mode is CMYK, not RGB
  • Pantone references noted for any brand colour that must match
  • 3 mm bleed on every edge; no white slivers
  • Text and logos at least 5 mm from the cut line
  • No important element over a crease or on the glue flap
  • Fonts outlined or embedded
  • Dieline on its own non-printing layer, not flattened
  • Foil / spot UV areas on separate spot-colour layers
  • Barcode has 4 mm quiet zone and correct magnification
  • Legal copy (ingredients, warnings, country of origin) present if required for your market

No artwork? Start with the dieline anyway

You can order without a designer. Send us the logo (even a low-res PNG) and the text you want on each panel; we place it on the dieline and send a PDF proof for approval. It is a simple typographic layout, not a brand identity — but it is print-ready, and it lets you hold a real sample before investing in design.

Frequently Asked Questions

What file format should I send for box printing?

AI or layered PDF is best, CDR is fine. 300 dpi at final size, CMYK colour, 3 mm bleed, fonts outlined, and the cut/crease lines on a separate non-printing layer. JPG and PNG are not usable for print production.

Can you print from a Canva design?

Yes, if you export a print PDF at 300 dpi with bleed, then check that the CMYK conversion did not shift your brand colours. Canva designs often use RGB colours and no bleed by default, so both need fixing before sending.

How much bleed do I need?

3 mm on every edge is our standard, extending past the cut line. It covers the ±1 mm tolerance of printing and die-cutting so no white edge appears on the finished box.

What if I do not have a designer?

Send your logo and the text for each panel — we lay it out on the dieline and send a PDF proof for approval. Simple but correctly set up, and it lets you approve a physical sample before paying for full design work.

How long does artwork approval add to the lead time?

Usually 1–2 days if the file is clean. If we have to fix bleed, resolution or colour mode, add another day or two. Sampling is 3–5 days after artwork approval, and bulk production 12–15 days after the sample is signed off.
