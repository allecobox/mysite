Box Printing & Finishes: What Each One Costs You

Printing is included in every price we quote. Finishing is where budgets drift. This page lists each option with the effect it creates, roughly what it adds, and the quantity below which it stops being worth it.

By AllecoBox · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Where the money goes: structure and board set the base price; printing adds 0–15%; lamination adds 5–12%; foil and spot UV add 15–35% each below 1,000 pcs; embossing adds a one-off die plus 10–20%. Order quantity is the only lever that lowers all of them at once.

Printing: CMYK or Pantone?

CMYK (four-colour process) mixes cyan, magenta, yellow and black dots to build an image. It is what you need for photographs, gradients and multi-colour artwork — and it is included in our quoted prices.

Pantone (spot colour) is a pre-mixed ink, one plate per colour. It is the only way to hit an exact brand colour, and the only way to print a solid, bright orange or a true metallic. It costs extra because each spot colour needs its own plate and wash-up.

A common compromise: print the photography in CMYK and add one Pantone for the logo only. That keeps most of the accuracy for a fraction of the plate cost.

What to skip below 500 pcs

Small runs carry fixed costs per finish, so the per-unit penalty is brutal. At 100–300 pcs we usually recommend:

  • Skip foil and embossing. The die alone can add more than the printing. A single Pantone metallic ink gets 80% of the look for a fraction of the cost.
  • Keep lamination. It is the cheapest way to make a thin carton feel solid, and it protects the print in transit.
  • Replace spot UV with a design trick. A flat matte background plus one glossy printed element reads as premium without a screen charge.
  • Use one pass instead of two. Printing outside only, with a plain inside, saves a full pass at low volume.

Finishes that fight each other

Some combinations look great on a sample and fail in production: foil over matte lamination needs a primer or it will not adhere; spot UV on soft-touch needs a special varnish or it wipes off; embossing across a crease cracks on corrugated. We check the combination against the board before quoting, so you hear about the conflict before a die is made — not after.

The 3-finish rule

Good packaging usually has one base surface treatment, one accent, and one structural feature — for example matte lamination + gold foil on the logo + a magnetic closure. Adding a fourth effect rarely reads as more premium; it reads as busier, and it costs more than the base box. If your budget is tight, spend it on structure (a rigid box) rather than on more finishes applied to a flimsy one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is printing included in your quoted price?

Yes. Every price we quote includes full-colour CMYK printing on your artwork, plus the free structural dieline. Pantone spot colours, special finishes and inserts are quoted as additions, so you can see exactly what each one costs.

How much does gold foil add to a box?

Roughly 20–35% of the unit price per stamped area below 1,000 pcs, because a foil die and a separate pass are needed. Above 5,000 pcs the percentage drops sharply. A single metallic Pantone ink is a cheaper alternative if you only need the colour, not the reflective stamp.

Can I print inside the box?

Yes — inside printing is common on rigid gift boxes and premium mailers, and it is a strong unboxing moment. It adds a full print pass, typically 10–20% of unit price, and needs the inside artwork supplied on a separate layer of the same file.

What is the difference between matte lamination and soft-touch?

Both are films; matte lamination gives a smooth, non-reflective surface at +5–8%, while soft-touch uses a velvet-textured film at +10–15%. Soft-touch feels noticeably more expensive in the hand but scuffs more easily with handling, so it suits boxes that are presented rather than shipped repeatedly.

Do finishes change the MOQ?

Yes, in practice. The box structure sets the official MOQ (100 pcs for 9 of our 15 types), but foil, embossing and window patching carry their own setup costs, so they only make economic sense from about 300–500 pcs. Below that, we suggest cheaper substitutes with a similar look.
