What Is a Dieline? A Plain-English Guide

A dieline (also spelled die-line or die line) is the flat, unfolded drawing of your box. It tells the printer where to cut, where to crease, and where your artwork is allowed to sit. Get it right once and every reprint is identical — get it wrong and you pay for a second print run.

By AllecoBox · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

In one sentence: a dieline is a technical drawing of your box laid flat, with solid lines for cuts, dashed lines for creases, a 3 mm bleed outside the cut line, and a safe area inside it where text and logos must stay.

What a dieline actually contains

Think of a dieline as a cookie cutter flattened out. The die-cutting machine follows the solid lines with a steel blade and presses the dashed lines with a blunt rule to make folds. Everything else on the sheet — your artwork, your logo, a barcode — is printed first, then cut and folded to match.

Reading the line types

LineWhat it meansWhat you do in artwork
Solid lineCut — the blade goes through hereNothing crosses it except the bleed background
Dashed lineCrease / foldKeep text away; the fold will crack heavy ink coverage
Dotted line, inside the outlineSafe area (usually 5 mm in from the cut)All text, logos and barcodes stay inside
Outer band, past the outlineBleed (usually 3 mm)Extend background colour and images 3 mm past the cut line
Hatched areaGlue flapNever put design-critical content here — it is glued and hidden
Perforated lineTear strip or pop-outTreat as a feature: keep branding clear of the tear path

Bleed and safe area, in plain numbers

These two numbers cause more reprints than anything else:

  • Bleed: 3 mm. Printing and cutting are accurate to about ±1 mm. If your background stops exactly on the cut line, a 1 mm drift leaves a white sliver along the edge. Extending the artwork 3 mm past the line hides it.
  • Safe area: 5 mm. Text closer than 5 mm to a cut edge looks like a mistake, and on a folded box the crease can shave the top off your letters.
  • Fold clearance: 3 mm each side. Black text straddling a crease will crack along the fold on corrugated board, even with lamination.

How to get a dieline from us

  1. Send the internal size and the product. A photo or a sketch is enough — we just need to know what goes inside and how it is presented.
  2. We send a free dieline in 24 hours. Vector PDF or AI, to scale, with the internal dimensions annotated. It is yours to keep, whether you order or not.
  3. Place your artwork on the dieline layer. Keep our cut and crease lines on a separate, non-printing layer (usually named "dieline" and set to a spot colour called "Dieline"), so the printer knows not to print them.
  4. Send back the layered file. AI, PDF or CDR. We check the bleed, the safe area and the fold lines, and flag anything that will not print well before it goes on press.

If you have no artwork at all, we can lay out a simple typographic design on the dieline for you — that is usually the fastest route to a first sample for a small brand.

Five dieline mistakes that cost money

  1. No bleed. Background stops on the cut line → white hairline on every edge. This alone can force a reprint.
  2. Text over a crease. Small type on the fold splits on corrugated and cracks on coated board.
  3. Artwork on the glue flap. That 12–15 mm strip is folded inward and glued — anything printed there is never seen.
  4. Wrong orientation. A design that reads correctly on the flat sheet can end up sideways once the box is folded. Check the front panel label on the dieline, not the sheet.
  5. RGB files at 72 dpi. Web images look fine on screen and print flat and pixelated. Everything print-bound should be 300 dpi CMYK.

Dieline vs template vs mockup

Three different things, often confused: a dieline is the cutting/creasing drawing; a template is that drawing with placeholder artwork zones; a mockup is a rendered 3D image of the finished box. We send all three with the first quote, so you can check the structure, place your design, and see how it will look on a shelf before committing to a sample.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to pay for a dieline?

No. Structural dielines are free, in vector PDF or AI, delivered within 24 hours of your enquiry. They are yours to keep whether or not you place an order — there is no charge and no obligation.

Can I use my own dieline?

Yes. Send it as AI, PDF or CDR with the cut and crease lines on a separate layer or spot colour. We check it against our press tolerances before printing; if something will not fold or cut cleanly, we tell you before production rather than after.

What is the difference between bleed and safe area?

Bleed is 3 mm of artwork extending past the cut line so no white edge appears when the die drifts by ±1 mm. Safe area is 5 mm inside the cut line where text and logos must stay so they are not clipped or cracked by a fold.

How much does a custom dieline cost if my shape is not standard?

A fully custom die is a one-off tooling cost, typically quoted separately and only once. For most projects one of our 15 standard structures already fits, which means no tooling charge at all and a much lower MOQ.
