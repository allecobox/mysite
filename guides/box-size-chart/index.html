Standard size or custom size?

Standard sizes save money only when they fit well. A box 20 mm too large wastes board on every unit and lets the product move in transit; a box 3 mm too small fails at the packing line. Because our dielines are free and cut to the millimetre, changing 187 mm to 190 mm costs nothing — the only real constraint is the MOQ, not the size.

Watch the dimensional weight

Couriers charge by whichever is greater: actual weight or dimensional weight, which is (L × W × H in cm) ÷ 5,000 for most international express services. A box that is 2 cm too big on each axis can add 20–30% to shipping on light products. Right-sizing is usually the fastest saving in a packaging project — often more than switching suppliers.

Example: 30 × 22 × 8 cm = 5,280 cm³ ÷ 5,000 = 1.06 kg dimensional weight. At 700 g actual weight, you pay for 1.06 kg. Trim 2 cm off the length and you drop below the 1 kg bracket.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these box sizes internal or external?

Internal. All dimensions in the chart are inside measurements, because that is what your product has to fit into. Outside dimensions are larger by twice the board thickness on each axis — roughly 1 mm for a folding carton up to 13 mm for double-wall corrugated.

Can I order a size that is not on the chart?

Yes. Every box is cut to your size at no extra tooling cost when it uses one of our 15 standard structures. Send the internal dimensions in mm and we cut to the millimetre; the chart is only a shortcut for common products.

What if my product sits between two standard sizes?

Always round up, then reduce the clearance rather than the box. Going up one size costs a few cents of board; going down risks the product not fitting or being damaged in transit. If it is a close call, we can cut an exact size in between.

How do I reduce shipping cost with box size?

Right-size the box so the dimensional weight stays under the next courier bracket: dimensional weight = (L × W × H in cm) ÷ 5,000. Trimming 1–2 cm off each axis often drops a light parcel into a cheaper weight band, and it also reduces board usage.
