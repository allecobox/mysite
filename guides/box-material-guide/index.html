Box Material Guide: Choosing the Right Board

The same box shape made from three different boards looks like three different products — and costs three different prices. Here is how corrugated, greyboard, art paper and kraft actually differ, with the thicknesses and price bands we quote every day.

By AllecoBox · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Quick rule: corrugated for shipping and protection, greyboard (rigid) for premium unboxing, art paper / folding carton for retail shelf at volume, kraft for a natural sustainable look, food-grade board whenever the box touches food directly.

Corrugated: flute letters explained

The letter is the wave height. Bigger flute = more cushioning but less printable surface and a bigger box footprint for the same internal size.

FluteThicknessProtectionPrint qualityUse it for
E1.5–1.8 mmLightGood — near-offset smoothnessMailers, retail-style shipping boxes, e-commerce
B2.8–3.2 mmMediumGoodSubscription boxes, product shipping, medium weight
C3.6–4.0 mmHighFairTransport boxes, stacked pallets, fragile goods
BC double wall5.8–6.5 mmVery highFairExport cartons, heavy or high-value shipments

E-flute is the sweet spot for direct-to-consumer brands: it prints almost like card, folds like card, and still survives a courier. Move to B or C flute only when the product needs real cushioning.

Rigid greyboard: what makes a gift box feel expensive

A rigid box is a greyboard core wrapped in printed paper. It does not fold flat — it arrives assembled, which is why it costs more and why its MOQ starts at 500 pcs. What you get for that:

  • Weight and rigidity — the box holds its shape when opened with one hand, which is most of the "premium" feeling
  • Crisp edges — 1.5 mm board wrapped tightly gives a sharp corner that folding cartons cannot match
  • Finishing headroom — foil, embossing and soft-touch lamination all behave better on a wrapped rigid surface
  • Reusability — customers keep rigid boxes, which extends brand exposure long after delivery

1.5 mm is standard for jewellery and cosmetics; 2 mm and above for wine, spirits and large presentation sets where the lid must not flex.

Food contact: what "food-grade" really means

If the box touches food directly — pastries, burgers, chocolate, loose tea — the board must be food-contact compliant, either unprinted virgin fibre or coated with a food-safe PE/PLA layer. Two practical points:

  • Recycled board is not automatically food-safe. Printing inks from recycled fibre can migrate; for direct contact we use virgin food-grade stock.
  • Soy and water-based inks are the default on food packaging. Tell us if you need a specific compliance certificate for your market (EU 1935/2004, FDA 21 CFR).

Sustainability, without the greenwash

What actually helps, in order of impact: right-sizing the box (less material and cheaper shipping), single-material construction (no plastic window, paper-based insert), FSC-certified board, water-based or soy inks, and avoiding lamination where the finish allows it. A kraft box with one-colour print and no lamination is close to the lowest-impact option we can make — and one of the cheapest.

What sounds good but does not help much: "biodegradable" claims on a box that is laminated with plastic film, and recycled-content numbers quoted without the virgin fibre needed for strength.

Mixing materials inside one box

Most premium boxes combine two materials: a rigid greyboard shell with an EVA or cardboard insert, or a corrugated mailer with a printed paper sleeve. That is normal and often cheaper than making the whole box rigid. Send us the product and we will tell you the minimum material mix that still gives the unboxing you want.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between corrugated and rigid boxes?

Corrugated board has a wavy flute layer between two liners, so it is light, cushioning and ships flat. Rigid (greyboard) boxes are a solid board core wrapped in printed paper — heavier, stiffer, delivered assembled, and noticeably more premium. Corrugated starts at 100 pcs; rigid gift boxes at 500 pcs.

Which flute should I choose for an e-commerce mailer?

E-flute for most direct-to-consumer mailers: it prints almost like card and survives courier handling. Move up to B-flute if the product is over about 1 kg or fragile, and to C-flute or double wall only for heavy or export shipments.

Is kraft board cheaper than printed white board?

Usually similar or slightly cheaper, because kraft hides scuffs and often needs less coating. The saving is real but modest — most of the price difference between boxes comes from structure and finishing, not from switching to kraft.

Can I print full colour on corrugated board?

Yes, but the flute texture shows through. E-flute takes full CMYK well; B and C flute need a litho-laminated top sheet for photo-quality images. For crisp small text and fine gradients, folding carton or rigid board is the better choice.

Do you offer FSC-certified or recycled board?

Yes — FSC-certified and high-recycled-content boards are available on request, with the certificate number printed or stated on the quote. Tell us at enquiry stage, as certified stock can add a few days to the lead time.
