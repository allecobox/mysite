Fold boxes in detail: one-piece cartons, lock tabs and folding sequenceRead more

Every box on this page is cut from one piece of board and folds into shape without glue — that is the definition that separates a fold box from a tray or a lid-and-base pair. The 183 layouts cover donut and burger boxes with a flip lid, popcorn cartons with a criss-cross base, flip-top cartons with an easy-lock insert, and corrugated shipping versions of the same idea. Because there is no glued seam, the assembly happens at the point of use, which is why the fold sequence has to be obvious: a box that needs training to erect costs more in labour than it saves in material.

Three things decide whether a fold box behaves. Crease quality, because a poorly scored line on 350 gsm board will crack the print on the outside of the fold. Lock tab depth, because a 12 mm tab holds and an 8 mm tab slips once the box is full. And base construction, since a criss-cross base carries far more than a simple tuck base at the same board weight. We send a folded sample rather than a flat dieline whenever the structure is new to you, because the sequence is easier to show than to describe.
